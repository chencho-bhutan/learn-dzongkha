Categories of Alphabets: Where a Letter Sits Matters

We've met individual letters, vowels, and compounds. Now: where a letter sits relative to the sound-producing letter in a syllable — because in Dzongkha, position isn't just visual, it's meaningful.

Dzongkha organizes every letter in a syllable into four positional categories.

མིང་གཞི། — Mingzhi (The Root Letter) — 20 letters

The Mingzhi is the single most important letter in any syllable: it's the letter that actually produces the sound of the syllable. Everything else is defined relative to this letter — before it, after it, or after that.

སྔོན་འཇུག་ལྔ། — Ngonjug (Prefix Letters) — 5 letters

Ngonjug means "that which precedes." These 5 letters can sit before the Mingzhi:

ག་ད་བ་མ་འ

A prefix letter doesn't change how the syllable sounds when read aloud — but it's still written, and plays a role in the word's grammar and history.

རྗེས་འཇུག་བཅུ། — Jejug (Suffix Letters) — 10 letters

Jejug means "that which follows." These 10 letters can sit after the Mingzhi:

ག་ང་ད་ན་བ་མ་འ་ར་ལ་ས

Unlike prefixes, suffix letters often are pronounced, closing out the syllable's sound.

ཡང་འཇུག་གཉིས། — Yangjug (Post-Suffix Letters) — 2 letters

Yangjug means "that which follows again" — these 2 letters can appear after a Jejug suffix:

ད་ས

Seeing it all together: འཇིགས

Let's break down a real word, syllable by syllable:

འཇིགས
ཇ Produces the syllable's sound མིང་གཞི — Mingzhi
འ Comes before the main letter སྔོན་འཇུག — Ngonjug
ག Comes after the main letter རྗེས་འཇུག — Jejug
ས Comes at the very end ཡང་འཇུག — Yangjug
ི The attached vowel (ee) དབྱངས — Vowel

Every piece of this one written syllable has a defined job — nothing is arbitrary, even though at first glance a word like འཇིགས looks like a dense cluster of unrelated letters.

Why this matters

At this point, you've met every fundamental building block of the Dzongkha writing system: གསལ་བྱེད (the 30 consonants), དབྱངས (the 4 vowels), མགོ་ཅན / འདོགས་ཅན (compounded letters), and now སྔོན་འཇུག, མིང་གཞི, རྗེས་འཇུག, ཡང་འཇུག — the four positional categories. Every word in Dzongkha is built from these, combined according to the rules we've covered.
