Vowels: དབྱངས་བཞི། (The Four Yaang)

So far we've met consonants — the "frame" sounds of Dzongkha. Now we meet their partner: vowels, called Yaang (དབྱངས་). Unlike consonants, which Dzongkha has 30 of, there are only four vowels — but no consonant can form a spoken syllable without one attached.

Why vowels matter

Every consonant we've learned so far — ཀ, ཁ, ག, ང, ཅ, ཆ, ཇ, ཉ — actually already carries a built-in, "default" vowel sound when written alone. What the four Yaang do is change that default sound to something else, by attaching a small mark above or below the consonant.

This means learning these four vowel marks unlocks four new pronunciations for every consonant you already know. Instead of 30 sounds, you're suddenly working with well over a hundred possible syllables — all built from the same small set of pieces.
