Meet the Ucen Script: The 30 Consonants

Now that we know where the alphabet fits in our roadmap (Step 1), let's meet it. Dzongkha is written in the Ucen script — the same script used for classical Tibetan and Choke. It has 30 consonants, traditionally taught in rows of four. You don't need to memorize all of them today — just get your eyes used to seeing them.

ཀ་ཁ་ག་ང་

ka — kha — ga — nga

ཅ་ཆ་ཇ་ཉ་

ca — cha — ja — nya

ཏ་ཐ་ད་ན་

ta — tha — da — na

པ་ཕ་བ་མ་

pa — pha — ba — ma

ཙ་ཚ་ཛ་ཝ་

tsa — tsha — dza — wa

ཞ་ཟ་འ་ཡ་

zha — za — 'a — ya

ར་ལ་ཤ་ས་

ra — la — sha — sa

ཧ་ཨ་

ha — a

Notice the pattern

Each row is a "family" of related sounds, grouped by how the sound is produced in the mouth. Row 1 (ka, kha, ga, nga) is made at the back of the throat; Row 3 (ta, tha, da, na) is made with the tongue at the front of the mouth. You don't need the technical linguistics — just notice the grouping, which makes these far easier to hold onto than 30 random shapes.

A note on romanization: some of these consonants don't have an obvious English equivalent, and systems vary in how they spell them out. Don't worry about perfect pronunciation from text alone — we'll go row by row with audio.
